# Code of Conduct

> **Las mejores ideas nacen en ambientes donde el conocimiento puede cuestionarse con respeto.**

---

# Purpose

This Code of Conduct defines the expected behavior of every contributor participating in the BHG-Knowledge ecosystem.

The objective is to create a professional environment where ideas, evidence and continuous improvement are valued above hierarchy or ego.

---

# Our Culture

We believe that:

- Knowledge is our most valuable asset.
- Every idea deserves to be evaluated on its merits.
- Constructive disagreement strengthens systems.
- Transparency builds trust.
- Long-term quality is more important than short-term speed.

---

# Expected Behavior

Every contributor should:

- communicate respectfully;
- explain reasoning;
- support claims with evidence whenever possible;
- remain open to feedback;
- accept corrections professionally;
- preserve documentation quality;
- think about long-term consequences;
- contribute honestly.

---

# Unacceptable Behavior

The following behaviors are not acceptable:

- harassment;
- discrimination;
- intimidation;
- personal attacks;
- intentional misinformation;
- plagiarism;
- destruction of repository history;
- unauthorized removal of documented knowledge;
- abuse of contributor privileges.

---

# AI Participation

Artificial Intelligence systems participating in this repository shall:

- operate within repository governance;
- identify assumptions when certainty is limited;
- avoid fabricating information;
- preserve human decisions;
- prioritize traceability over creativity when documenting knowledge;
- assist contributors without replacing accountability.

Final responsibility always belongs to the human contributor.

---

# Decision-Making Principles

When disagreements occur, decisions should prioritize:

1. Evidence.
2. Repository standards.
3. Long-term maintainability.
4. Strategic alignment.
5. Simplicity.
6. Reproducibility.

Authority alone should never replace reasoning.

---

# Knowledge Ethics

Contributors should:

- document failures;
- preserve lessons learned;
- acknowledge uncertainty;
- avoid hiding mistakes;
- credit external sources appropriately.

Knowledge improves when both successes and failures are preserved.

---

# Reporting Concerns

Concerns related to:

- repository integrity;
- contributor conduct;
- documentation quality;
- governance violations;
- security concerns;

should be reported following the repository governance process.

---

# Continuous Improvement

This document is expected to evolve.

Changes shall improve clarity, fairness and collaboration without compromising the repository's guiding principles.

---

# Final Principle

Respect is demonstrated not by avoiding disagreement, but by discussing ideas with honesty, evidence and professionalism.

The goal is not to win arguments.

The goal is to build better systems.
